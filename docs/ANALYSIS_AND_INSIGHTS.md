# Analysis and Insights

## 1. National POS Spending

The long-run series shows Saudi POS spending rising between May 2020 and August 2026, with substantial variation from week to week. Average valid weekly spending rose from SAR 7.49B during the partial May to December 2020 period to SAR 14.37B during the partial January to August 2026 period. The latest observation, for the week ending 15 August 2026, records SAR 14.19B in spending. It remains below the series high of SAR 20.10B on 29 March 2025, so a single peak should not be treated as the underlying trend.

Elevated weeks recur throughout the series and are often followed by pullbacks. The latest week is one example: spending decreased 2.8% from the previous available week, while transactions decreased 1.5%. These movements should be interpreted cautiously. The dashboard shows when a spike or decline occurred, but calendar events, economic conditions, and payment behavior would require additional evidence before they could be offered as explanations.

## 2. Transactions vs Average Ticket

The central relationship is:

> **Total Spending = Transaction Count × Average Transaction Value**

What stands out in this relationship is that transaction volume grew much faster than average purchase value. Average valid weekly transactions increased from 62.93M in the partial 2020 period to 240.06M in the partial 2026 period. Over the same comparison, average weekly spending increased from SAR 7.49B to SAR 14.37B, while the weighted average ticket declined from SAR 118.96 to SAR 59.85.

The latest week reinforces this pattern: 249.68M transactions generated SAR 14.19B, producing a weighted average ticket of SAR 56.84. The long-run change is consistent with spending growth becoming more volume-driven than ticket-driven. It does not explain why consumers are making more transactions or why average ticket values have moderated.

The ticket measure is weighted correctly as total transaction value divided by total transaction count. It is not an average of weekly or subgroup averages, which could distort the result.

## 3. City Concentration

The city analysis uses a stable comparison group of ten named cities. In the latest week, Riyadh leads at SAR 4.84B, followed by Jeddah at SAR 1.88B and Dammam at SAR 0.65B. Makkah and Madinah each contribute approximately SAR 0.57B. Khobar, Buraidah, Abha, Tabuk, and Hail together contribute SAR 1.44B.

Riyadh accounts for 48.6% of the SAR 9.95B recorded across these ten cities. This indicates substantial concentration within the ten-city cohort. **Share of Tracked Top 10 Cities is not a national POS market-share measure.** The denominator excludes activity outside the ten named cities and therefore should not be interpreted as Riyadh's share of all Saudi POS spending.

The fixed cohort supports a consistent historical comparison. Historical `Other`/`Others` records are excluded because the places represented by that residual category changed over time.

## 5. Digital Shift

The channel comparison shows physical POS remaining larger in the latest comparable month while mada e-commerce grows faster. In June 2026, physical POS sales were SAR 57.48B and mada e-commerce sales were SAR 33.63B. mada therefore represented 36.9% of their combined sales value.

The difference is larger in transaction counts: physical POS recorded 1,009.24M transactions, compared with 184.78M mada e-commerce transactions. mada represented 15.5% of the combined transaction count. The lower transaction share than sales share indicates a higher average transaction value in mada e-commerce for this month, but the dashboard does not identify the customer, merchant, or product-level reasons.

Compared with June 2025, mada e-commerce sales increased 29.5% and transactions increased 30.5%. Over the same comparison, POS sales increased 6.5% and POS transactions increased 9.8%. mada e-commerce grew faster over this period, while physical POS remained larger in both value and volume.

## 6. What the Dashboard Can Tell Us

The dashboard can be used to examine:

- weekly national POS spending and transaction-volume trends;
- changes in weighted average ticket and previous-available-week performance;
- concentration and historical patterns across ten consistently tracked cities;
- the current top-level merchant-category mix;
- monthly physical POS and mada e-commerce channel shifts; and
- unusual periods that merit further investigation.

## 7. What It Cannot Tell Us

The dashboard cannot determine consumer motivations, individual household behavior, income changes, or the cause of a specific weekly movement. It does not cover cash, bank transfers, or every other form of Saudi consumer spending. It also cannot make the legacy and current merchant taxonomies comparable, and it should not be used to infer national city market share from the ten-city cohort.

This version is descriptive and diagnostic. It contains no predictive model and provides no evidence about future outcomes. Forecasting would require a separately defined target, chronological validation, external calendar or economic features where justified, and explicit treatment of uncertainty.
